### Notable events after the elections (popular front victory), in cronological order ###

# Feb 16, The Popular Front wins the General Elections.
# Feb 17, Assault on right-wing party headquarters and newspapers.
# Feb 17, The right-wing leader José María Gil-Robles asks Portela to declare a state of war, which he refuses to do.
# Feb 18, Government meets and decides to declare a state of alarm for eight days.
# Feb 19, Azaña forms a new Goverment, excluding the socialists (PSOE)
# Feb 21, New Amnesty Law for the reprisals of the Revolution of 1934.
# Feb 23, General López Ochoa is arrested for his responsibility in the repression of October 34.
# Feb 26, The Generalitat of Catalonia is restored and its Parliament re-elects Lluis Companys.
# Feb 26, Franco is named commander general of the Canary Islands to keep him away from the Madrid conspiracies.
# Feb 28, General Emilio Mola is removed from his post as head of the African Army, in the Spanish protectorate of Morocco, and assigned to Navarre.

# Mar 2, Preparations begin in Caspe to draw up a 1936 Statute of Autonomy of Aragon.
# Mar 10, Attack against the vice-president of the Cortes, the Socialist Luis Jiménez de Asúa, by a Falangist militant, in which his bodyguard is killed.
# Mar 14, Falange Española is declared illegal as antidemocratic and its founder, José Antonio Primo de Rivera, is arrested.
# Mar 16, Decree confiscating the large estates returned to their owners in Andalusia.
# Mar 25, Occupation of estates in Extremadura by some sixty thousand peasants.

# Apr 7, Alcalá-Zamora, President of the Republic, resigns after a motion of censure. Diego Martínez Barrio takes office provisionally.
# Apr 13,  The magistrate of the Audiencia Manuel Pedregal, who had condemned those who attacked Jiménez de Asúa, is assassinated by Falangists.
# Apr 14, Serious incidents in the Paseo de la Castellana during the parade commemorating the proclamation of the Republic, where Anastasio de los Reyes, ensign of the Civil Guard, dies at the hands of the Socialists, and two other agents are wounded.
# Apr 19, Failed attempt of military uprising in Madrid led by Rodríguez del Barrio. The coordination of the conspiracy will pass to Emilio Mola.
# Apr 16, Serious incidents take place during the funeral of Ensign Anastasio de los Reyes, when the funeral procession is shot at by gunmen in the streets of Madrid, causing skirmishes throughout the city. A unit of the Assault Guard under the command of Lieutenant Castillo dissolves the march with the use of firearms. In the incidents, the Falangist Andrés Sáenz de Heredia, cousin of José Antonio Primo de Rivera, dies and the serious wounding of a Carlist militant is attributed to the lieutenant.
# May 29, In Yeste (Albacete) a harsh confrontation took place between day laborers and the Civil Guard.

# May 3, Azaña is elected new president of the Republic.
# May 10, Azaña is proclaimed president of the Republic. 2 days later Santiago Casares de Quiroga forms goverment.
# May 20, Preparations of the draft bill of autonomy for Castilla y León begins (Not realiable info, so treat this lightly)
# May 20, The Government decrees the closure of religious schools with the argument of preventing them from being assaulted.

# June 2, A construction strike begins, paralyzing almost all construction sites and taking on revolutionary overtones. It ends on July 4 with a government arbitration that satisfies the strikers. However, it was prolonged even longer due to fierce clashes between ugetistas and cenetistas.
# June 16, A tough parliamentary session on public order, between Calvo Sotelo, Gil-Robles, Casares Quiroga and Dolores Ibárruri.
# June 23, Franco sends a letter to Casares Quiroga informing him of the existence of a coup conspiracy.
# June 25, The Statute of Euskadi is approved for its approval in Congress.
# June 28, A referendum is held in Galicia in which the vast majority is in favor of the Statute of Autonomy.

# July 11, In Valencia a group of Falangists assaults a radio station and launches fascist proclamations.
# July 12, Lieutenant José Castillo, of the Assault Guard, member of the Republican Antifascist Military Union and instructor of the Socialist Youth militia, is shot to death by a platoon of four Falangists.
# July 13, Calvo Sotelo is assasinated by socialists in vengance of the murder of José Castillo.
# July 13, The Socialist leader, Indalecio Prieto, asked Casares to distribute weapons among the workers in the face of the threat of a coup, which he refused to do.
# July 13, In view of the tension created, the Government orders the closure of the headquarters of political groups related to anarchism and Carlism.

#### Start of the Insurrection ####

## July 17, Morocco:
# Meeting of Colonel Soláns and lieutenant colonels Seguí, Gazapo and Bartomeu in the Boundary Commission of the old Alcazaba Barracks in Melilla. They are going to elaborate the strategy to be communicated to the Falangist chiefs for the occupation of the public buildings the following day. The news reached the ears of General Manuel Romerales, Military Commander of Melilla, who sent a patrol of Assault Guards to search the cartographic department for weapons. The also present legionary lieutenant Julio de la Torre Galán telephones the nearby Legion barracks to ask for help, and about twenty legionaries show up and point guns at the assault guards, who finally join the rebels. Barracks after barracks, the order is given to advance the uprising.
# Colonel Soláns along with other rebel officers enter Romerales' office, forcing him to resign at gunpoint. Soláns replaces him in command.
# Bartomeu declares a state of war in Melilla at 17:00 in the name of General Franco "General in Chief of the Moroccan Forces". The uprising begins early.
# Legionnaires and regulars were deployed in Melilla, occupying the main government buildings and arresting leftist militants, trade unions and Freemasons. By nightfall, the city is in the hands of the rebels.
# The President of the Government was informed of the events in Melilla and alerted General Agustín Gómez Morato, head of the Army of Africa, who was in Larache. He flew to Melilla to pacify the rebels, but was stopped by the rebels as soon as he landed.
# Warned by Seguí, colonels Eduardo Sáenz de Buruaga and Juan Yagüe declare a state of war in Tetuán and Ceuta respectively. Both cities were soon taken.
# Lieutenant Colonel Yagüe takes control of Ceuta with the support of the Legionnaires and arrests Lieutenant Colonel Caballero Lopez, head of the regulars, and loyal to the Government.

## July 17, Peninsula:
# The government orders the concentration of bombers at the Tablada airfield (Seville), to attack the African barracks at dawn.
# The Minister of the Navy, José Giral orders the immediate blockade of the Rif with destroyers and cruisers.
# The Goverment orders the arrest of General Varela, in Cádiz, who is sent to prision.

## July 18, Morocco:
# Captain Moreno Farriols declares a state of war in the last important city of Morocco loyal to the Government, Larache. At dawn it will be in rebel hands. The chief of this zone, Lieutenant Colonel Romero Basart, refuses to join the uprising and manages to flee to French Morocco.

## July 18, Canarias:
# Franco decrees the uprising in the Canary Islands at 5:15 in the morning. At 7:10 a.m. he sends a telegram to the Army of Africa declaring its adherence to the uprising.
# In La Palma the general strike is declared and popular militias are formed, keeping the island under the republican legality. It is the well known Red Week. (https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semana_roja_(Espa%C3%B1a))

## July 18, Armada:
# The battleship Jaime I and the destroyers Sánchez Barcáiztegui (SB) and Almirante Valdés (AV) tried to switch to the Nationalists, but the sailors rose up and prevented it. On board the cruisers Libertad and Miguel de Cervantes, the officers were arrested, among them Vice Admiral Angel de Mier, head of the cruiser division. The officers of the submarines showed a totally passive attitude towards the uprising, for which they were also arrested.
# The destroyer Churruca (CH) joined the rebels. Together with the ship Ciudad de Algeciras, she sailed for Cadiz with regimental troops.

## July 18, Andalucía:
# In Seville, General Gonzalo Queipo de Llano, Inspector General of Carabineros, appeared at the headquarters of the 2nd Division. Thanks to the collaboration of the officers, he detained General Fernandez de Villa-Abrille, proclaimed a state of war and ordered the barracks of the city to place themselves under Queipo's command.
# Between 14:30 and 15:00 Queipo de Llano gives the signal to the remaining garrisons of Andalusia to rise up. Throughout the afternoon, those of Cadiz (General Jose Lopez-Pinto), Jerez de la Frontera (Commander Salvador de Arizon), Algeciras (Lieutenant Colonel Manuel Coco), Cordoba (Colonel Ciriaco Cascajo) and Malaga (General Francisco Patxot) seconded the call.
# The Civil Government of Seville surrenders to the attacks of the rebels, who take control of the center of the city.
# In the afternoon, a column of miners loaded with dynamite left Riotinto for Seville to quell the uprising. A group of civil and assault guards sent from Huelva to protect the column arrived in Seville at dawn, changed sides and joined the rebels.

## July 18, Center and North:
# The Government dismissed Generals Franco, Queipo de Llano and Cabanellas and ordered the arrest of military coup plotters in Zaragoza, Burgos and Vitoria.

## July 18, Rest of the Country:
# At 8:00 a.m. the Government informs on the radio about the uprising in North Africa. The Announcement: https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Alocuci%C3%B3n_del_Gobierno_sobre_un_movimiento_militar_en_Marruecos_de_18_de_julio_de_1936,_Madrid
# The UGT and the CNT declare a general strike throughout Spain.
# Socialists and Communists radioed for "arms for the people" and called on their faithful to go to their locals to prepare for a "possible confrontation".  The Generalitat of Catalonia refuses, as well as several civil governors. In Madrid 5000 rifles from the Artillery Park are distributed and in San Sebastian columns of militiamen are formed and marching towards Vitoria.

### July 19 ###
# General Franco, after spending the night incognito in Casablanca, flies to Tetouan in the Dragon Rapide and takes command of the Army of Africa.
# The uprising is generalized throughout most of the peninsular territory.
## On July 18 at 22:00 Santiago Casares Quiroga, head of the Republican government, resigns. Azaña entrusts the formation of a new government to Diego Martínez Barrio, with the mission of negotiating an agreement with the rebels. Mola rejects the peace offer, so Martínez Barrio resigns at 8:00 on the 19th. José Giral forms a government, which orders the delivery of weapons to the people.
### Andalucía ###
# The destroyer Churruca disembarks the regulars in Cadiz under the command of Commander Oliver. With these forces, General López Pinto took control of the Civil Government and the City Hall and took control of the city. During the return trip, the crew of the ship revolted against their superiors and returned under the control of the government of the Republic.
# Corporal Espartel, escorted by the Dato, moved a tabor (battalion) of regulars to Algeciras. The rebels take control of the city.
# In Malaga, after heavy fighting, the rebels withdrew to their barracks. The city is plunged into chaos for several days and is in the hands of the Government.
# A column of Malaga militiamen, heavily armed, headed for the Gibraltar countryside. It will be stopped in San Roque.
# In Granada, General Miguel Campins declares his loyalty to the Government but refuses to give arms to the people. Meanwhile, the chief of the Armilla airfield, loyal to the Government, flees with his staff to Almeria.
### Madrid ###
# In the morning, weapons begin to be distributed to the people.
# The arrest of Colonel Galarza and the resignation of General Villegas leaves the leadership of the uprising in Madrid to General Fanjul, who installs himself in the Cuartel de la Montaña at 12:30. The barracks are surrounded at nightfall, beginning the Siege of the Cuartel de la Montaña.
### Center & North:
# In Valladolid, the commander of the 7th Organic Division, General Molero Lobo, was shot and wounded in his office by pro-coup officers. General Saliquet took command and declared a state of war. The rebels quickly took control of the city because the leftist militants had no weapons.
# Almost all the territory of the 7th Division (provinces of Valladolid, Segovia, Avila, Zamora, Salamanca and Caceres) fell under the control of the rebels. Only Béjar (Salamanca), Requejo (Zamora) and some towns in Avila, especially Arenas de San Pedro, resisted.
# In Burgos the commander of the 6th Organic Division, General Batet, is arrested by the coup plotters led by Colonel Gavilán, who take control of the city. A state of war is declared in Palencia and Logroño.
# In Pamplona General Mola declares a state of war at dawn.
# The rebels declare a state of war in Zaragoza, take control of the city and arrest the Civil Governor and more than 300 leaders of trade unions and left-wing political parties.
# A column under the command of Colonel Francisco García-Escámez leaves Pamplona heading for Madrid, after having the Civil Guard declare a State of War in Soria, it arrives in Guadalajara, which has already been conquered by the Republicans. In view of this, he moved to Somosierra.
### Balearic Islands ###
# General Goded, head of the Balearic Islands Military Command, declares a state of war in Palma de Mallorca at 7:30 am and at 10:30 am leaves by seaplane for Barcelona.
# General Bosch, military commander of Menorca, declares a state of war in Mahon, but is finally reduced by his NCOs (Non-Commisioned Officers).
### Barcelona ###
# The military uprising in Barcelona begins at 4:00. It is provisionally led by General Álvaro Fernández Burriel. General Francisco Llano de la Encomienda, head of the 4th Organic Division, remains loyal to the Government.
# After heavy fighting, the uprising is crushed in Barcelona by the leftist militias, the Guardia Civil, the Guardia de Asalto and the Air Force.
### Asturias ###
# In the morning, two columns of miners left Oviedo for Madrid, with the assurance of Colonel Antonio Aranda Mata, head of the Exempt Military Command of Asturias, that there were no signs of military rebellion in the region. However, throughout the day Aranda refuses with excuses to deliver weapons to the people and at 17:00 he declares himself in favor of the uprising. The Civil Government surrenders at nightfall.
### Murcia ###
# The Civil Guard of Albacete revolts and takes over the city.
# A group of Falangists fail in their attempt to free Primo de Rivera from prison in Alicante.

### July 20 ###
# General Sanjurjo, who was to become Head of State according to the plans of the rebels, dies in a plane crash when the plane taking him back to Spain from his exile in Estoril took off.
# In Madrid, the assault on the Cuartel de la Montaña and the surrender of the barracks of Campamento and Vicálvaro put an end to the uprising.
# Alcalá de Henares (Madrid) is taken by rebel officers who had insubordinated their commands.
# Vigo, La Coruña and the naval base of Ferrol fall into the hands of the rebels despite the resistance of the leftist militants and the fact that the sailors of the battleship España and the cruiser Almirante Cervera mutinied and remained loyal to the Government. In Vigo the fighting continued for several days in the popular neighborhoods.
# Part of the garrison of Gijón (Asturias) revolted but was quickly surrounded in its barracks, beginning the siege of Gijón.
# The military garrison of Granada revolted at 5:00 p.m. and quickly took control of most of the city, the Armilla airfield and the El Fargue explosives factory. This was not the case of the Albaicín, a mostly working class neighborhood that resisted the attacks of the rebels.
# Between the afternoon of the 19th and the morning of the 20th, the tabor of Regulares de Ceuta arrive in Seville from Cádiz and another company of Regulares arrives from Algeciras; as well as by air a complete company of the Legion, commanded by commander Castejón. In the afternoon the legionnaires attacked Triana and failed. They did manage to seize Dos Hermanas, the last obstacle in the terrestrial communication between Seville and Cádiz. Three government planes bomb the Tablada airfield, without causing any significant damage.
